This is the planning and pseudocode doc for the Kill Your Neighbor card game 

1. The deck of cards needs to be defined. 
2. Each card's value/assignment needs to be defined. 
3. The game loop needs to be defined, this includes card actions like jokers += 5 cards and being able 
    to stack, 
    Kings being able to reverse the path, 
    Jacks changing the accepted suit for that hand, 
    2's and jokers with their penalties,
    what a losing score is, 
    what a starting hand is, 
    defining each card for display,
    etc. 
4. Within each function, action, verb, etc. there needs to be a print statement advising what action/s are
    taking place behind the scenes. <==== This is the crux of the assignment and where we will demonstrate 
    learning and comprehension. 
5. Each hand needs to end with checking how many cards each player has; game ends when someone hits 0.
6. Game needs to display who won and provide option to continue or not. 

First, the program needs to define, deal and display those cards for the player, and deal the 
same amount of cards from the same "deck", so that of the ~54 cards, the game knows what player 
has, what CPU has, and remains in the deck.

The program needs to take a card from the remaining deck and have it be the starting card; 
player should chose from their hand what to play in response to the first card, and CPU will 
play next. The game cannot start without the first card being selected and displayed.

The game needs to evaluate what is valid and what is not, as well as if any player has 0 cards.
The goal is to run out of cards each round, since the first player to reach x00 points loses 
the game as a whole. 

There are rules for certain cards:
2: can be played on 2 of any suit, recipient draws 2 cards if they cannot add a 2 to the pile. 
    These can stack. 
4: skips the next player in line; in a 2 player game, this brings the play right back to the 
    player who uses a 4. These can be played back to back to back.
Jack: allows player to select the suit, but player must wait a round before they can play 
    another card; the risk is the suit being different by the time it comes back around.
King: Reverses the flow of the game. Gameplay with more than 2 players is "clockwise".
    These can be played back to back to back.
Joker: Draw 5; these can stack. 
The rest of the cards are incidental and played based on if they match the current suit or 
face (This is what a successful play validates against; the only cards that do not have to 
match NEITHER suit nor face are Jacks and Jokers)

Each group of cards has its own point assignment: 
2-9 = 5 points
10, J, Q, K = 10 points
Aces = 40 points
Jokers = 50 points
